# The games that come with Kraken

Ten games ship with Kraken, so a table can play with nothing else installed:
Kraken's own **Descending Darkness** and **Apeira Astra**, and eight from open
licences: **D&D 5E** (the SRD 5.2.1), **Basic Fantasy**, **Cairn**,
**Ironsworn**, **Starforged**, **Fate Core**, **Fate Accelerated** and
**Forged in the Dark** (from the Blades in the Dark SRD). Each has its rules
book, its GM's material, and a first adventure with story, encounters and maps
(with walls and doors). Make a campaign with the game as its ruleset, then
make its books active in **Modules**. Each open game's text keeps its own
licence; see its folder's `LICENSE.md` under `games/`.

## D&D 5E: the SRD 5.2.1

The System Reference Document 5.2.1 as three books for a D&D 5E campaign,
made from the SRD's own PDF:

- **SRD 5.2.1 Player's Rules** (a players' book): every rules chapter as
  pages (Playing the Game, Character Creation, Classes with their subclasses
  and spell lists, Character Origins, Feats, Equipment, Spells, Rules
  Glossary, Gameplay Toolbox), and what the character wizard, the sheet and
  the tracker use: 12 classes with a subclass each and their features to
  level 20, 9 species (the Elf's lineages, the Tiefling's legacies, the
  Dragonborn's ancestors), 4 backgrounds, the feats, every weapon, armor,
  tool and piece of gear, and 339 spells. Spells that roll (attacks, saves
  with half damage, damage and healing that grow with the slot or the
  caster's level, Magic Missile's darts, Bless's d4) play on the Combat
  Tracker.
- **SRD 5.2.1 Creatures** (the GM's): all 330 monster and animal stat blocks,
  by creature type, as NPCs whose attacks roll on the tracker.
- **SRD 5.2.1 Magic Items** (the GM's): the Magic Items chapter and its 258
  items, for parcels and the party sheet.

There is no adventure and no art (the SRD has none). Your own 5E modules and
packs sit beside these books. The books carry the SRD's licence notice:

## Descending Darkness (dark fantasy)

The d20 family: roll a d20, add a modifier, beat a number. What makes it its
own:

- **Fragile heroes.** Characters go to level 10, not 20. Health is lower, and
  at 0 Health a character is **dying and bleeds** 1 Health at the start of each
  of their turns until someone steadies them (**Steady** in the combat
  tracker: a Wisdom check against 12) or they die. There are no death saves.
- **Dread.** A track from 0 to 6 on the sheet. Horrors, the dark and the dead
  raise it; at 3 a character is Shaken, and worse follows.
- **Kin, classes, backgrounds.** Five kin (Human, Elf, Half-Elf, Dwarf,
  Gnome), each allowing some of the seven classes (Fighter, Cleric, Wizard,
  Thief, Paladin, Ranger, Bard), and eight backgrounds.
- **Standard array** 17, 14, 14, 13, 12, 8; Defense instead of AC.

**Books:** *Descending Darkness Player's Handbook* (rules, kin, classes, 36
spells, gear), *Descending Darkness Bestiary* (18 creatures, each with its
portrait and token).

**Adventure: *The Empty Watchtower*** (levels 1 to 2, one or two sessions).
The beacon on the ridge above Hollowmere has gone dark, and the dogs will not
stop howling. Ten story entries, six encounters, three maps (the yard, the
upper floor, the crypt) and three handouts. The way through is not only a
fight: Aldous's journal says how the Warden can be relieved of his watch.

## Apeira Astra (science fiction)

Its own 2d6 engine:

- **Tasks.** Roll 2d6 + skill + characteristic modifier against **8**. The
  margin is the **Effect**: it adds to damage and decides how well things go.
  Difficulty adds or takes away (Easy +2, Hard -2, and so on); a **Boon** rolls
  3d6 and keeps the best two, a **Bane** the worst two. Untrained is -3.
- **Six characteristics:** Brawn, Reflex, Grit, Intellect, Training, Presence.
  **Stamina** is Brawn + Reflex + Grit. **Armour** subtracts from damage.
  A character is **Down** at 0 Stamina and dead at minus their Grit.
- **A crew in minutes.** Choose an **Origin** (Coreborn, Spacer, Frontier,
  Undercity, Belter), a major **Path** (Fleet, Marine, Scout, Merchant,
  Engineer, Medic, Drifter: its signature skill and four picks) and a minor
  Path (two picks). No levels: experience buys skills and characteristics
  (no skill above 4, no characteristic above 15).
- **Marks and tricks.** The GM awards a **Mark** when a crew member does
  something the port will repeat (at most six in a career). Each Mark teaches
  one **trick** from the character's major or minor Path (three each: Berth
  Hands, First Through, Fair Price, Stay With Me...). The sheet keeps them: the
  Main page shows the Marks and the tricks with their text, the GM's **Award a
  Mark** gives one, and the player picks the trick; a skill a trick names shows
  it beside the skill.

**Books:** *Apeira Astra Crew Handbook* (rules, Origins, Paths with their
tricks, Marks, every piece of gear and its price, salvage law), *Apeira Astra
Threat Dossier* (22 threats and contacts, each with its portrait and token; a
salvage roll, how to run a job, and six jobs ready to play).

**Adventure: *Salvage Rights*** (a new crew, two sessions). The freighter
*Kestrel's Due* is adrift in the Heron belt, and a rival salvager is racing
the crew to it. But someone aboard may still be alive, and that changes
everything. Ten story entries, four encounters, two deck maps with hatches,
and four handouts.

**Adventure: *A Lamp in the Rock*** (a new crew, one session). A freighter
fused with an asteroid, two stamps on one salvage claim, a rival cutter on the
clock, a passenger still alive, and a case that should not be spun. Nine story
entries, five encounters, two maps with walls and hatches, and a handout.

## Ironsworn and Ironsworn: Starforged (vows, with or without a GM)

Two rulesets on one engine: **Ironsworn** (with *Delve*: perilous sites) and
**Starforged** (a spacefarer in the Forge). Kraken plays them from Shawn
Tomkin's CC BY 4.0 material: the moves, assets and oracle tables (and
Delve's themes and domains, Starforged's sample foes and setting truths),
taken from Datasworn and kept only where their licence is CC BY 4.0. The full
rulebooks are CC BY-NC-SA and are not included: the books' rules chapters
are Kraken's own short explanation. This is an unofficial adaptation,
compatible with the games, not a Tomkin Press product.

- **The action roll:** an action die (d6) + a stat + adds, at most 10,
  against two challenge dice (d10): a strong hit beats both, a weak hit one,
  a miss neither; a tie goes to the die; both dice the same is a match.
- **Momentum** (-6 to +10, starting at +2): burn it when it beats more dice
  than your score (the sheet offers it before the outcome is resolved); it
  resets to +2 (+1 with one debility or impact, 0 with more). Negative
  momentum cancels an action die of its value.
- **Progress tracks:** vows, journeys, expeditions, fights, connections and
  sites, ten boxes of four ticks, marked by rank (troublesome 3 boxes ...
  epic 1 tick); a progress roll is the boxes against the challenge dice.
- **Experience:** Ironsworn's from fulfilled vows; Starforged's from the
  quests, bonds and discoveries legacy tracks (2 a box). A new asset costs 3,
  an asset's next ability 2.
- **The sheet:** every move on the **Moves** tab (its option, adds, the
  track or foe it is made against); what an outcome plainly does is applied,
  its choices are buttons. **Oracles** in the sidebar for everyone; **Delve
  Sites** for the GM (Ironsworn).
- **The combat tracker** shows foes as a rank and a progress track, and each
  character's initiative (Starforged: in control or in a bad spot).
- **No money:** supply is shared by the party; a parcel's **Supply** refills
  it.

**Books (Ironsworn):** *Ironsworn Reference* (how to play, 48 moves with
Delve's, 78 assets, 86 oracle tables), *Ironsworn Foes and Sites* (14 foes of
Kraken's own, Delve's 8 themes and 12 domains), *The Cinder Barrow* (a
starter: an inciting vow, a haunted barrow with walls and doors).
**Books (Starforged):** *Starforged Reference* (how to play, 56 moves, 87
assets, 262 oracle tables, the setting truths), *Starforged Foes* (55 sample
foes), *The Silent Hulk* (a starter: a derelict colony ship with walls and
doors).

## Cairn, Second Edition (dark fairy-tale fantasy)

Yochai Gal's adventure game about exploring a dark and mysterious Wood. Its
rules, as the book has them:

- **Three attributes and Hit Protection.** STR, DEX and WIL (3d6 each, swap
  two once); HP 1d6. A **save** is a d20 at or under the attribute: a 1 always
  succeeds, a 20 always fails. Roll them from the sheet (the d20 by each
  attribute).
- **Attacks always hit.** The weapon's die, less the target's **Armor** (3 at
  most), comes off HP. Two weapons (d6+d6) roll both and keep the best;
  **impaired** attacks roll d4, **enhanced** d12; **blast** hits everyone in
  the area. Damage past HP comes off **STR**, and the target saves STR (with
  the new score) or takes **Critical Damage**: a PC can only crawl until
  someone gives aid (**Stabilize**), a creature is defeated. STR 0 is death,
  DEX 0 paralysis, WIL 0 delirium. Damage that takes a PC's HP to exactly 0
  rolls on the **Scars** table (Kraken rolls its dice; a scar that improves
  you once you recover has a **Recover** button on the Growth tab).
- **The combat tracker** plays Cairn's rounds: **Sides (DEX saves)** puts the
  PCs first, each saving DEX to act in the first round, then their opponents.
  The GM's **Morale** asks a WIL save to stand (Kraken asks it by itself at a
  side's first casualty and when half have fallen). Creatures marked ⚑ are
  **detachments**: an individual's attacks on them are impaired, theirs are
  enhanced and blast.
- **Ten slots.** Four on the body or in hand, six in the backpack; bulky
  items take two, petty none, each **Fatigue** one. A full inventory takes HP
  to 0. Casting from a **spellbook** adds a Fatigue (in danger, a WIL save
  first); a **scroll** is gone once read; a **relic** spends a charge.
- **Rest:** a short rest brings HP back, a night's rest also clears Fatigue,
  a week with a healer brings STR, DEX and WIL back. A **deprived** character
  recovers nothing.
- **No levels, no experience.** Characters grow from what happens to them:
  the sheet's **Growth** tab keeps the Scars, what has changed and Downtime
  milestones, and the party sheet's Growth tab writes a change on anyone.
- **The character wizard** is the book's Character Creation: a background
  (rolled or chosen) with its names, gear and two tables, the attributes, HP,
  the traits, a Bond, the age, the youngest's Omen, and 3d6 gold pieces at
  the Marketplace.

**Books:** *Cairn Player's Guide* (the rules, the twenty backgrounds, the
Marketplace, Vald), *Cairn Warden's Guide* (the Warden's chapters, the
bestiary's 84 creatures, the 100 spellbooks, the reliquary's 46 relics, and
its tables to roll in the Library).

**Adventure: *Trouble in Twin Lakes*** (CAS-1). The butcher of Isthmus Town
has vanished, and a man was seen swallowed by the earth near Deadmill. Story
entries for every place and person, encounters (night cats, bandits, the
Surarus), the adventure's relics and treasure, and two battle maps Kraken
drew from its text: the Temple of the Fungal Lizards and Deadmill.

## Fate Core and Fate Accelerated (any genre)

Evil Hat's Fate, in its two versions: **Fate Core** (skills) and **Fate
Accelerated** (approaches). Make a campaign with either ruleset; each has
its players' book, and both share the GM's book and the starter scenario.

- **Four Fate dice and the ladder.** A roll is 4dF (each die plus, minus or
  blank: −4 to +4) plus a skill or an approach, read on the ladder:
  Terrible −2, Poor −1, Mediocre +0, Average +1, Fair +2, Good +3, Great +4,
  Superb +5, Fantastic +6, Epic +7, Legendary +8. The dice tray has a Fate
  die (dF: four of them make a roll); the 3D dice, the tower and chat show
  them as plus, minus and blank, and chat gives the total on the ladder.
- **Four actions, four outcomes.** On the sheet pick the action (overcome,
  create an advantage, attack, defend) and the opposition, then a skill's
  **4dF** button: fail, tie, succeed, or succeed with style (by 3 or more),
  with the shifts. Create an advantage makes its aspect with a free invoke
  (two with style, a boost on a tie); an overcome with style gives a boost.
- **Aspects and fate points.** A high concept, a trouble and three more;
  consequences are aspects too. After a roll, **+2** or **Reroll** on any
  aspect spends a fate point; a scene aspect's free invoke costs none.
  Compels: **Accept** gains a fate point, **Refuse** pays one. Refresh is 3,
  less one for each stunt past three; **Refresh** (or the GM's **Refresh
  all**, a new session) brings fate points back up to it.
- **The Scene Aspects strip** over the table shows everyone the scene's
  situation aspects, boosts and game aspects with their free invokes, and
  the GM's pool of fate points. The GM's tools on it (⚙): add an aspect,
  change its free invokes, **New scene** (the pool set to one fate point a
  player character, the last scene's aspects gone), **Refresh all**.
- **Stress and consequences.** Fate Core: a physical track from Physique and
  a mental one from Will (two boxes; Average or Fair adds a 3-box, Good or
  Great a 3 and a 4; Superb an extra mild consequence); Fate Accelerated:
  three boxes. A hit's shifts are taken by one stress box (a box of n takes
  n) and any free consequences (mild 2, moderate 4, severe 6, extreme 8).
  Consequences recover: **Recover**, then **Clear**.
- **Conflicts on the combat tracker.** Choose a physical or mental conflict;
  **Turn order** sets it without dice (Notice, then Athletics; Fate
  Accelerated Quick; mental Empathy or Careful). An attack from the sheet
  (the action Attack, at your target) or an NPC's skill on the tracker rolls
  against the target's best defence. An NPC takes a hit the lightest way, or
  is **taken out**; a hit on a player's character waits on the tracker until
  its player (or the GM) picks how to take it and names the consequences, or
  takes them out. **Concede** leaves the conflict on your own terms, for a
  fate point and one more for each consequence taken. A tie gives the
  attacker a boost; a defence with style, the defender. The end of the
  conflict clears everyone's stress.
- **Milestones, not experience.** The sheet's Milestones tab (and the party
  sheet's, for everyone): minor (switch two skills, rename an aspect, swap a
  stunt), significant (a skill point to spend), major (a refresh more).
  Fate has no coin: a parcel's finds are written into each character's
  **extras**.
- **The character wizard** follows each book: Fate Core's high concept and
  trouble, the phase trio (an aspect each), the skill pyramid (one Great, two
  Good, three Fair, four Average) and stunts from the book's skills or your
  own; Fate Accelerated's aspects, approaches (+3, +2, +2, +1, +1, +0) and
  stunts. Or take a pregenerated character as it is.

**Books:** *Fate Core System* (the SRD's every chapter; the 18 skills and
their 54 example stunts as records), *Fate Accelerated Edition* (the SRD's
every chapter; the six approaches, its example stunts, and its four example
characters to play), *Fate Toolkit and Adversaries* (the GM's: the Fate
System Toolkit and the Fate Adversary Toolkit, and the SRDs' example
opposition as NPCs for the tracker: nameless, supporting and main NPCs from
Fate Core, mooks from Fate Accelerated, the Adversary Toolkit's enemies).

**Starter scenario: *The Lighthouse at Gull's End*** (Kraken's own, for
either game). The light on the point has gone dark, and ships are breaking
on the rocks. Story entries with each scene's aspects, two conflicts
(wreckers and their chief on the strand; a smuggler captain in the lamp
room), two battle maps with their zones outlined in colour, and three
pregenerated characters in both games.

## Forged in the Dark (a crew of scoundrels, CC BY 3.0)

Based on Blades in the Dark (John Harper, One Seven Design) through its
System Reference Document. The SRD has the rules and the means to make
playbooks and crews, but no setting and no finished playbooks: Kraken adds
its own city of **Brackwater** (a canal port of salt fog and gaslight), seven
playbooks and six crew types built from the SRD's example abilities, the
standard items and a starter score.

- **Actions and attributes.** Twelve actions (Hunt, Study, Survey, Tinker /
  Finesse, Prowl, Skirmish, Wreck / Attune, Command, Consort, Sway); an
  attribute's rating is how many of its actions have a dot.
- **Action rolls.** Click an action on the sheet: its dots are d6s on the
  table's dice, the highest one read (6 success, two 6s critical, 4-5 partial,
  1-3 bad; with no dots, two dice and the lowest). The GM's **position**
  (controlled, risky, desperate) and **effect** (zero to extreme) are on the
  roll and on its card in chat. Add a teammate's **assist** (+1d, 1 stress for
  them), **push** yourself (+1d or +1 effect, 2 stress each) or take a
  **Devil's Bargain** (+1d). A desperate roll marks xp in the action's
  attribute. A roll can tick a clock by its effect.
- **Resistance** (click an attribute): 6 stress less the highest die; a
  critical clears 1. **Armor** boxes (armor, heavy, special) reduce a
  consequence instead. Stress past the last box is **trauma**; the fourth
  retires the scoundrel.
- **Harm**: two lesser, two moderate, one severe (a full row moves harm up;
  past the top it is fatal), with their penalties (less effect, -1d, need
  help). Recovery fills the **healing clock**; full, every harm is a level
  less.
- **Downtime** (the GM begins and ends it on the Score panel): payoff, heat
  (at 9 a wanted level), the entanglement roll, then two activities each (more
  for a coin or a rep): acquire an asset, a long-term project, recover, reduce
  heat, train, indulge your vice (overindulging when you clear more stress
  than you had).
- **The crew** (the party sheet's Crew tab): Tier, hold, rep and turf, heat
  and wanted level, coin and its vault, crew xp and advances, special
  abilities, upgrades, contacts, cohorts (gangs and experts), the claim map
  and faction status.
- **Progress clocks** (the Clocks window, and the top bar for every seat):
  danger, racing, linked, tug-of-war, mission, project and faction clocks.

**Books:** *Forged in the Dark: Scoundrel's Handbook* (every player chapter of
the SRD; the playbooks Enforcer, Tracker, Tinker, Burglar, Grifter, Schemer
and Occultist; the crew types Assassins, Bravos, Cult, Hawkers, Shadows and
Smugglers; heritages, backgrounds, vices, standard items),
*Forged in the Dark: Game Master's Guide* (the faction game, entanglements,
NPC and faction downtime, cohorts, magnitude, rituals, crafting, making your
own playbooks and crews).

**Starter score: *The Brine Street Job*.** A fence hires the crew to lift a
guild's true ledger from a canal-side counting-house before dawn: story
entries, four factions of Brackwater, its people, four clocks to put on the
table from the Clocks window, its payoff, and the Brine Street
Counting-house map with its walls and doors.

## Basic Fantasy (old-school fantasy, CC BY-SA)

The **Basic Fantasy Role-Playing Game, 4th Edition** by Chris Gonnerman
(www.basicfantasy.org): classic old-school play, free and open. In Kraken:

- **The character wizard follows the book.** Roll 3d6 for each ability in
  order (the host's dice, in chat; the GM may allow exchanging scores);
  choose a race whose limits the scores meet (Dwarf, Elf, Halfling, Human)
  and a class it may take with its Prime Requisite of 9 (Cleric, Fighter,
  Magic-User, Thief; an Elf may be a Fighter/Magic-User or a
  Magic-User/Thief); roll the class's hit die + Constitution; roll 3d6 x 10
  gp and buy equipment from the book's list (the shop greys out what the
  class or race may not use: a Cleric's edged weapons, a Thief's metal armour,
  a Halfling's large weapons); a Magic-User picks a first spell besides Read
  Magic; name and alignment.
- **The sheet** works everything out: ability bonuses, ascending Armor Class
  (armour + shield + Dexterity), the attack bonus (melee with Strength,
  missile with Dexterity), the five saving throws by class and level with the
  race's bonus, movement by armour and load, thief abilities, spells a day,
  turning the undead. Click a saving throw, an ability (the book's optional
  ability roll), a thief ability or loyalty to roll it in front of the table.
  The level up button rolls the hit die to 9th level and adds the class's
  fixed hit points after. A Human gets +10% experience from the party sheet.
- **The combat tracker** plays the Encounter rules: initiative d6 +
  Dexterity, highest first; an attack is d20 + attack bonus at or above the
  target's AC (a natural 20 hits, a 1 misses); a monster attacks with the
  bonus for its hit dice, claw, claw, bite; saves at or above the number (a
  natural 1 fails); anyone at 0 hit points is dead. A Cleric's **Turn undead**
  rolls d20 against the table, 2d6 hit dice affected. The GM's **Morale**
  button rolls 2d6 against a monster's morale.

**Books:** *Basic Fantasy Player's Handbook* (the player's chapters: making
a character, races, classes, equipment, spells, adventuring and encounters;
4 races, 6 classes, 105 spells and 85 items as records) and *Basic Fantasy
Game Master's Guide* (245 monsters as NPC records, 135 magic items, treasure
and the Game Master's chapters).

**Adventure: *The Mill at Hob's Ford*** (1st level, one or two sessions;
written for Kraken). Goblins have moved into the cellars under the village's
mill, and they have woken something older below. Ten story entries, three
encounters (goblins, their hobgoblin chief, skeletons to turn), three treasure
parcels, two maps (the mill, the cellars with a secret door) and the miller's
note as a handout.
